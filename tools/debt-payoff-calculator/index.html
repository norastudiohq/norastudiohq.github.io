Debt payoff questions, answered

Debt snowball vs avalanche: which is better?

The avalanche (highest interest rate first) always costs the least interest, or ties. The snowball (smallest balance first) pays off your first debt sooner, and those quick wins keep many people going. If the two are close, as they often are, pick the one you'll stick with. This calculator shows the exact gap for your debts.

How do I calculate my debt-free date?

Each month, add interest to every balance (APR divided by 12), pay every minimum, and put all the extra money on one target debt. When a debt hits zero, its minimum joins the extra and rolls to the next. Count the months until every balance is zero. That's what this calculator does for you.

Why does "minimums only" say it will never be paid off?

If a minimum payment is less than the interest charged each month, the balance grows instead of shrinking. Raise that payment, add extra money, or look into a lower-rate option such as a balance transfer.

How much extra should I pay each month?

Any amount helps, because extra money goes straight to principal. Try a few amounts and save each one to your list: you'll see how many months and how much interest every extra $50 or $100 saves.
