How this calculator handles odd numbers

So you can trust the total, here is exactly what it does with input that is not a clean number.

  • Blank rows stay blank. A line counts only when it has a quantity above zero and a unit price. Until then the line total is empty rather than $0.00, so an unfinished quote does not look like a pile of zeroes. A unit price of 0 is allowed, so you can show an item as included at no charge.
  • Dollar signs and thousands commas are fine. Type $1,250.50 and it reads 1250.50.
  • Letters and negative numbers are ignored, not guessed. A quantity or price that is not a plain positive number (text, a minus sign, a negative quantity) makes that line count as nothing, and the row border turns red so you can see it. It never adds a wrong amount to your subtotal. To show money going the other way, use the discount box instead of a negative quantity.
  • The discount can never be bigger than the subtotal. A flat discount larger than the work is capped at the subtotal, and a percentage is capped at 100%, so the total never goes below $0.00.
  • Tax is charged after the discount. Subtotal, minus discount, then tax on what is left. That is the usual order on a trade quote, but check your own local rule if you discount often.
  • Everything is rounded to the nearest cent, with a half cent rounding up, at each step: line total, subtotal, discount, tax, total and deposit. The deposit and the balance always add back up to exactly the total.

Quote and estimate questions, answered

What is the difference between a quote and an estimate?

A quote is a fixed price you are offering to stand behind: if the customer accepts it, that is the number on the invoice. An estimate is your best guess at what the job will cost, and everyone understands it can move once the walls are open or the scope grows. Use the same calculation for both, but say in writing which one you are sending, and if it is an estimate, say what would make it change.

Do I add tax before or after the discount?

In most places the discount comes off first and tax is charged on the discounted amount, because tax follows the price actually paid. This calculator works that way: subtotal, minus discount, then tax on the remainder. Sales tax and VAT rules differ by country and sometimes by state, so if you discount a lot it is worth confirming your local rule once and then sticking to it.

How much deposit should I ask for?

For trade and service work, somewhere between 25% and 50% up front is normal, and the usual reason is materials: the deposit should at least cover what you have to buy before you start. For a small job, many people take nothing up front and invoice on completion. Whatever you pick, write it on the quote, because a deposit nobody agreed to in advance is the most common reason a first invoice gets argued over.

Should my quote show the tax separately?

Yes. Show the subtotal, the tax amount with its rate, and the total as three separate lines. A customer who sees only one big number has no way to check your maths, and a business customer reclaiming tax needs the amount broken out. The copied text and the CSV from this page both keep the three lines separate.

How long should a quote stay valid?

Thirty days is the common default, and it protects you when material prices move. Put the expiry on the quote itself, in the notes line, rather than leaving it open ended. If a customer comes back after it has expired, you can still honour the old price as a goodwill gesture, which reads much better than being forced into it.
