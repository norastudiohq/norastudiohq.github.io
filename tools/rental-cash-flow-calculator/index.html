Rental property numbers, explained

What is a good cap rate for a rental property?

Cap rate is NOI divided by the purchase price, ignoring the loan. Many investors look for roughly 5% to 10%. Lower cap rates (4-6%) are common in expensive, stable markets; higher ones (8-10%+) usually come with older properties or riskier areas. Compare cap rates between similar properties in the same market rather than against one fixed number.

What is a good cash-on-cash return?

Cash-on-cash return is your annual cash flow divided by the cash you put in (down payment plus closing costs). Many buy-and-hold investors target 8% to 12%. It reflects your financing, so a bigger down payment or higher interest rate changes it even when the property is the same.

What is the 1% rule?

The 1% rule is a quick screen: monthly rent should be at least 1% of the purchase price (a $250,000 house should rent for $2,500 a month). Passing it does not guarantee positive cash flow and failing it does not rule a deal out; run the full numbers above.

How do I estimate maintenance costs?

A common rule is 5% to 10% of rent for routine repairs, higher for older homes, or about 1% of the property value per year. Many investors also set aside a separate amount for big-ticket replacements such as roofs, HVAC and water heaters.

What is DSCR and why do lenders care?

Debt service coverage ratio is annual NOI divided by annual mortgage payments. A DSCR of 1.25 means the property earns 25% more than its loan payments. Many lenders want 1.2 to 1.25 or higher; below 1.0 the rent does not cover the mortgage.
