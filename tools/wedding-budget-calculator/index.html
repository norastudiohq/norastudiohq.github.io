Wedding budget questions, answered

How should I split my wedding budget?

A common starting point is about 45% for the venue plus catering and bar, about 14% for photography and video, 8% for flowers and decor, 8% for attire, hair and makeup, 5% for music, and the rest across rings, planner, stationery, rentals, cake, transport, favors and the officiant, with a few percent held back for surprises. Move money toward what matters most to you and away from what doesn't.

What is a normal cost per guest?

Divide your total budget by your headcount. Many US weddings land somewhere between $150 and $400 per guest all-in, and catering alone often runs $50 to $150 per head. If your cost per guest looks high, the guest list is usually the biggest lever.

How much does each extra guest cost?

More than the meal. Each guest adds food and drinks, a share of the cake, an invitation and postage, a chair, linens and place setting, and a favor. This calculator adds those per-head categories up so you can see what trimming or growing the list really does.

How much should I set aside for unexpected costs?

Plan on 5% to 10%. Taxes, service charges, gratuities, overtime and last-minute fixes are the usual surprises. Keep the "Contingency / other" row, or raise it if your quotes don't yet include tax and tips.
