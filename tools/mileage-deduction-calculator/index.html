2026 mileage deduction questions, answered

What is the 2026 IRS mileage rate?

For business driving it is 72.5 cents per mile for miles driven from January 1 through June 30, 2026, and 76 cents per mile for miles driven on or after July 1, 2026. The IRS raised it mid-year, which it rarely does, so a single-rate calculation gets the second half of the year wrong.

Which rate do I use for a trip in July?

The rate follows the date you drove. A trip on June 30 counts at 72.5 cents, a trip on July 1 at 76 cents. That's why this calculator asks for miles in each half of the year.

What if I only know my total miles?

Choose "only as a yearly total" and the calculator splits your miles by days: 181 of 365 days fall before July 1. That's an estimate. For your return, use your actual log, which should show each trip's date, purpose and miles.

Can I deduct parking and tolls too?

Yes. Business parking fees and tolls are generally deductible on top of the standard mileage rate. Gas, repairs, insurance and depreciation are not; the rate already covers them. Commuting from home to a regular workplace doesn't count as business miles.
